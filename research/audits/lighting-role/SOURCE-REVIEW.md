## Required sharper original contribution

Use a question such as: **What does a lighting artist hand over when another discipline can place a reusable light prop, and how is a bad-looking instance distinguished from an unsuitable preset or changed receiving environment?** This is a receiving and diagnosis interface, not just a version checklist. Proposed responsibilities below are original and unexecuted, not claimed studio practice:

- **Reusable prop/preset authoring:** distinguish visible fixture/emissive surface from the light component(s), intended visual role and project-authorized parameter/variant choices. The distinction is a proposed handoff field, not an engine implementation claim from these interviews.
- **Placement receiving:** identify who may position/select a variant and what nearby surface, obstruction or art-direction condition must be considered. Avoid universal department ownership or assuming a prefab can enforce artistic correctness.
- **Scene integration:** separately identify the surface response and ambient/time/fog/exposure context used to judge the placed instance. These are diagnostic dimensions, not a promised physically correct decomposition or an exhaustive combination grid.
- **A concrete decision fork:** before changing every instance of a shared preset, determine whether the intended correction concerns one placement, the receiving material/geometry, a global atmosphere/exposure policy, or the reusable preset. A shared edit can affect instances beyond the reported scene; this is ordinary dependency reasoning, not a measured failure in either studio.
- **Receiving outcome:** accept a local adjustment, a specifically scoped shared-preset revision, or an unresolved art-direction question. Record why rather than defaulting to “add another light.” Visual acceptance, technical cost and player comprehension still require their own evidence.

A compact worked hypothetical of a shared lamp used against two different surfaces/time states would be more distinctive than another doorway version table. Do not invent actual bright/dark outputs, shader physics, player detection or numerical budgets. The practical value is locating the change at the correct production level; whether that workflow reduces rework is unknown.
